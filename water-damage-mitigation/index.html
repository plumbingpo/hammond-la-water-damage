Mitigation means one thing: keeping the damage from getting any bigger. It is not the repair. It is everything done before the repair to protect what is still sound. For Hammond homes, that means we stop the source, pull the water out, seal off wet areas and get the structure stable. Good mitigation makes the later repair smaller, and now and then makes it unnecessary.

Many homeowners treat restoration and mitigation as one and the same. Each is its own phase of the job. During mitigation, the urgent part, the loss is halted, its spread is limited and the home is made stable. Restoration is what comes after: drying is confirmed, and damaged drywall, flooring and finishes are repaired or rebuilt. Insurers treat them as separate line items too, so it helps to know which phase you are in.

Four tasks make up most mitigation visits. Source control, so no new water enters. Extraction, so standing water is gone. Three: contain the area with plastic sheeting and managed airflow, so clean rooms stay free of moisture and contaminants. Stabilization, which means removing materials that cannot be saved, treating surfaces with antimicrobial, and setting air movers and dehumidifiers. Furniture and personal items are protected too, either relocated or raised on blocks.

Maintenance Tips for Water Damage Mitigation

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Have your water heater inspected yearly and replace it before the tank fails.
  • An automatic valve on the main line can cut the water as soon as it detects a leak.
  • Braided steel supply lines should take the place of rubber ones on the washing machine about every five years.
  • Once a month, look beneath sinks and near toilets for stains, drips or soft spots in the floor.
  • Clean the gutters regularly, and aim downspouts so they discharge away from the foundation.
  • Regrade low spots beside the house where water gathers after rain.

How to Tell You Need Water Damage Mitigation

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • The carpet pad is soaked through even where the surface seems only damp.
  • Your insurer has told you to take reasonable steps to prevent further damage.
  • The house feels muggy and window glass is wet with condensation since the leak.
  • After a leak, the baseboards have swollen or started separating from the wall.
  • Bathroom or kitchen cabinets are sitting over water that cannot be reached to dry.
  • A roof or window opening is letting rain into the building.
  • A musty odor has begun because materials stayed wet for a day.

Why Hire a Professional for Water Damage Mitigation?

Fast decisions, one after another, are what mitigation consists of. Can this section of drywall dry in place, or does it need cutting out? Is the water Category 1, 2 or 3, and has it changed since the leak began? Should the toe kick under the cabinets be pulled? Each decision rests on meter readings and our training, never on whether something seems dry to the eye or hand.

Your insurance claim benefits from proper mitigation too. Under most policies you must act reasonably to keep the damage from growing, with proof that you did. Arrival photos, daily moisture readings and an inventory of removed materials all go into our records. Together those records show the water's effect and our response to it. How We Handle Water Damage Mitigation

Our systematic approach delivers quality results on every project. Here's how we handle water damage mitigation from start to finish:

1

Control the Source

The supply gets shut off, the broken line capped, or the opening letting water in tarped and boarded. When the permanent repair calls for a roofer or plumber, we help schedule it.

2

Remove Standing Water

Standing water is removed with pumps and extractors, and carpet and pad are checked. Drying machines on their own cannot limit damage the way prompt bulk extraction does.

3

Set Up Containment

We isolate the affected rooms from the rest through airflow control and plastic barriers. This keeps humidity, dust and any contaminants from travelling through the house while work is under way.

4

Stabilize the Structure

Materials past saving come out, an antimicrobial is applied to surfaces, and air movers plus dehumidifiers go in. We log moisture readings each visit until the structure is ready for repairs, usually within 3 to 5 days.
